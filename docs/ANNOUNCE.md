**Title:** A free 3D app that shows a beginner what every rope and sail on a cruising yacht does (looking for corrections)

**Text:**

I am learning to sail and could not find a simple way to see what each rope, sheet and sail of a cruising yacht actually does. So I made one: a free web app with a 3D Hanse 508. You set a wind, work the ropes (a slider for each, or clutches and winches as on the boat), and see what the sails and the boat do. ## r/learntosail

Rules to read first: https://www.reddit.com/r/learntosail/about/rules

**Title:** A free app to see what the ropes and sails of a cruising yacht do before you step aboard

**Text:**

If you are new to sailing, the first day aboard is a lot of ropes. This is a free web app that lets you practise on a 3D model of a Hanse 508 first: set the wind, pull or ease a sheet, and watch the sails, the boat and the heel respond. Guided exercises with a coach cover the points of sail, tacking, gybing, reducing sail and leaving the berth. The Learn list explains every part and rope (what it is, what it does, typical mistakes).

It is a learning aid, not a replacement for real instruction. It is free, open source (GPL), runs in a phone's browser, and is made by one person with an AI coding assistant. Experienced sailors: I would be grateful for corrections.

https://botrops1.github.io/sail-learn-and-train/

Rules to read first: the category's pinned guidelines at https://discourse.threejs.org/c/showcase

**Title:** Sail: learn and train, a 3D cruising yacht that teaches how ropes and sails behave

**Text:**

A free, open-source (GPL) browser app built with three.js and TypeScript. It shows a beginner how the ropes, sheets and sails of a Hanse 508 behave: a rig solver turns wind and rope settings into sail forces, and the boat heels, steers and sails on simple waves, with a day-and-night sky, a marina and a bay. It runs on phones, with a phone-first layout and a coach for guided exercises.

It was made by one person with an AI coding assistant. I would welcome feedback on performance on your devices (add `?debug=1` to the address to see the frame rate), and corrections from sailors.
